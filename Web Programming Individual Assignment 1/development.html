Reflection on The Richest Man in Babylon

After reading the chapters "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" and "The Five Laws of Gold" changed how I think about money. I learned that financial success is not only about earning more, but also about knowing how to save, manage, protect, and grow what I have.

The Seven Cures taught me to save part of my lunch money, control unnecessary spending when I spend from my savings, prepare for the future, and improve my ability to earn. The part about controlling expenses stood out to me because it's easy to spend money on things I think I need.

The Five Laws of Gold reinforced the importance of saving and making careful financial decisions. I learned that I musn't invest in things I do not understand or chase opportunities that promise quick money. Instead, I should take the time to learn and make informed decisions.

I can apply these lessons by developing good financial habits while I am still a student. I can save what I can, spend carefully, and continue to improve my education and skills.

My biggest takeaway is that wealth is built through discipline and consistent habits, not simply by earning a lot of money. I do not need to wait until I have more money to start managing it properly.
